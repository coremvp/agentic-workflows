# Interviews and surveys

Load when a consequential uncertainty needs evidence from people. Start with
what decision an answer could change and what existing evidence cannot establish.
A research plan can finish without contacting anybody.

## Recruit for the situation, not agreement

Define inclusion by relevant recent behavior and constraints. Seek useful
contrasts: users who succeeded, abandoned, chose an alternative or do not fit.
Explain selection bias and any missing group. Choose effort/sample breadth for
the decision and available access; there is no universal interview count or
incentive. Outreach, incentives and recordings require their own authority and
privacy/consent decisions.

## Ask about what happened

Begin with an actual episode: “Walk me through the last time you sent work for
review.” Follow with what they used, where it failed, what happened next and what
the workaround cost. Ask for a concrete artifact only when sharing is permitted.
Separate the participant's language from your diagnosis.

| Leading prompt | More useful prompt |
| --- | --- |
| Would our instant approval button save you hours? | How did you know the last review was complete? |
| Don't you hate email feedback? | What works well about your current process? |
| Would you pay for this? | What did you choose last time, and what made that worth paying for? |
| Is this your biggest problem? | What would you fix before this, if anything? |

Test your favored explanation: ask what contradicts it, when the workaround is
sufficient and what would make a proposed solution unsuitable. Avoid explaining
our solution until the existing situation is understood. Prototype reaction is
not observed adoption; distinguish concept testing from task observation.

## Use surveys for questions they can answer

Define the eligible respondent and recall period. Ask one idea at a time, offer
meaningful alternatives plus “other/not applicable,” and avoid forcing invented
precision. Pilot wording for comprehension before broad distribution. A rating
without an episode often hides different meanings; pair it with a specific task
or explanation where useful. Minimize unnecessary personal data.

Report denominator, recruitment method, missing answers and wording. A voluntary
survey is not a representative market estimate. Distinguish exploratory themes
from prevalence claims; do not turn hypothetical willingness into revenue.

Deliver a decision-linked interview guide or survey, recruitment contrast,
disconfirming questions, interpretation plan and remaining limitations. Preserve
raw evidence through [customer research](customer-research.md); avoid automatic
research calendars, outreach or rigid quantitative gates.
