# Content strategy

Load to choose the next useful content work, rather than merely draft a known
artifact. Start from a reader decision the business can credibly help with.

## Identify the encounter and contribution

Read current product truth, audience evidence, existing pages/assets and actual
search/response signals. Where will a person encounter the answer, what do they
already know, and what are they trying to decide or do? A search troubleshooting
answer, a founder's observation in a feed and a setup guide have different jobs.
Topic relevance alone does not establish a reason to produce another artifact.

For a candidate, state its contribution: a missing explanation, source-backed
comparison, useful method, revealing example, demonstrated mechanism or recovery
step. What can this work add that the current owner or widely available answer
does not already provide? Use actual evidence and experience; novelty can be a
clearer explanation rather than an unsupported contrarian thesis.

## Inspect before adding

Find the existing answer and remaining gap. Choose among maintaining it,
clarifying a product page, teaching a supported task, explaining a real decision,
producing a proof-led demo or creating a genuinely missing owner. Distinct reader
jobs matter more than different keywords. SEO owns search intent and canonical
URLs; Writing owns the selected expression.

Before committing effort, sketch the thesis and evidence: what should the reader
understand or do afterward, what supports it, and which example could make it
useful early? If the argument depends on missing capabilities or invented proof,
route the product gap or narrow it. Sometimes improving documentation, fixing
the product or doing no new content is the strongest recommendation.

## Prioritize without fake precision

| Consideration | Question |
| --- | --- |
| Usefulness | What decision/action becomes easier for this reader? |
| Evidence | Are the claim and demand supported, or hypotheses? |
| Contribution | What useful understanding or proof does this add? |
| Business fit | Is the next action relevant to the supported offering? |
| Existing owner | Can an existing artifact do the job with a smaller change? |
| Proof/readiness | Can we show the mechanism honestly now? |
| Effort/distribution | Can we produce and place it where this audience encounters it? |

Use these as reasons, not invented weighted scores. A high-volume broad topic
can be weaker than a narrow obstacle blocking eligible users. A known incorrect
setup page may deserve repair before another feature announcement. A theme can
support a small connected body of work when each item has a distinct reader job;
it does not justify a calendar or page quota by itself.

For a fictional app with an existing invite guide, search excerpts about expired
links justify refreshing that guide if the missing recovery is supported. They
do not justify three new “invite troubleshooting” URLs or a weekly post quota.

## Produce a bounded brief

Recommend the next item or small ordered queue with encounter, reader job,
contribution/thesis, proof, existing or justified new owner, scope, effort, next
action and signal that could change the next choice. Explain why the strongest
alternative, including no new content, is weaker. Reuse the existing brief; do
not add another strategy workbook.

A campaign is appropriate when distinct artifacts do complementary jobs. Use
[content promotion](content-promotion.md) only when connected encounter planning
adds value. Keep response pending when none exists; a strategy does not establish
demand, traffic or customer learning.
