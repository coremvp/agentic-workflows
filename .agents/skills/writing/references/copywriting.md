# Copywriting

Load to express an accepted product/marketing argument persuasively and truthfully.
Marketing owns audience/argument; Product owns capability. Copy cannot repair
an unsupported offer by sounding more confident.

## Choose the starting point

For a blank brief, recover the reader's situation, encounter, desired progress,
current alternative, objection, available proof and useful action. State the
promise and central claim before drafting. If that decision is materially absent,
return it through Lead rather than quietly selecting a new audience or offer.

For an existing draft, inspect its intent, useful voice and argument first.
Name the failure and revise it through [editing](editing.md); preserve strong
phrasing and accepted choices. For a point edit, fix the requested passage and
check its surrounding meaning. None of these modes requires automatic variants,
a context database or a full rewrite.

## Build an evidence outline

Order the reader's unresolved questions. For each necessary section name its
job, claim and supporting fact, example or demonstration. Where proof is absent,
narrow the claim or make the missing dependency visible. Place decision-critical
proof beside the uncertainty it resolves; do not hide it in a final proof section.
Use [copywriting frameworks](copywriting-frameworks.md) only when shape is unsettled.

| Reader question | Useful expression |
| --- | --- |
| Is this my problem? | Specific situation/trigger |
| What changes? | Observable supported before/after |
| Why believe it? | Inspectable evidence near the claim |
| Why choose it over my workaround? | Relevant difference and tradeoff |
| What does trying it involve? | Actual eligibility, commitment, action and limits |

Open with a recognizable task, consequence or supported advantage, not a company
biography or generic urgency. For a fictional revision-history tool, “Keep
comments attached to their revision” is a specific association promise.
“Revolutionize collaboration” gives no decision help. The stronger promise still
needs its actual mechanism below it.

## Draft, then challenge the promise

Write the argument in connected prose before polishing isolated slogans. Show
how the mechanism produces the supported result. Revision-bound comments establish
context; they do not establish automatic approval or time saved. A demonstration
can answer “does this do the task?”; a real customer account can answer a different
question. Do not invent either or add unsupported facts while fixing weak wording.

A headline invites the reader into an argument the body must fulfill. Answer
the strongest real objection where it arises; use an FAQ only when it serves
distinct questions. Make the next action concrete and proportional to readiness,
including prerequisites, cost or commitment where material. A useful answer need
not end at checkout.

## Fit the medium and preserve character

A product page can support a decision with explanation and proof; an ad or short
post must select one supported idea. Technical docs serve action rather than
suspense. Match depth to reader knowledge, evidence and requested medium. Use
the founder's actual passages and relevant Taste to identify voice; borrowed
catchphrases and a list of adjectives are weak substitutes. Do not force every
artifact into the same recipe.

Revise truth/intent, section progression and repetition before sentence polish.
Ask whether each section changes understanding, confidence or ability to act,
then reread the complete changed passage for lost limitations and useful voice.
Deliver the requested draft with consequential assumptions, source/claim limits
and unresolved decisions. No simulated praise, conversion forecast or variant quota.
