# Editing

Load to revise a draft or inspect expression. Begin with the requested change,
reader job and source truth; preserve what already works.

## Diagnose before rewriting

Choose the scope. A blank or undecided brief needs the argument method in
[copywriting](copywriting.md); a draft with useful voice needs a targeted revision;
a point edit needs the smallest correction and a surrounding-context check.
Do not turn “make this sentence clearer” into a new positioning exercise.

Read as the intended reader, then state the consequential mismatch: missing
reason to care, weak proof, wrong order, vague noun, repeated information,
inaccurate promise, tonal mismatch or difficult action. Identify strong material
to keep, including purposeful humor, tension, rhythm or technical detail. A draft
may need one sentence repaired rather than a new voice.

| Check | Concrete question | Useful correction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Truth | Does the evidence establish this exact actor, behavior and scope? | Narrow/remove it or attach the missing limitation |
| Value | Does the reader know why this matters now? | Open with their task/consequence |
| Argument | Is each step earned? | Repair missing reason, contrast or proof |
| Specificity | Could this sentence describe any product? | Name actual behavior and useful detail |
| Sequence | Must the reader wait for necessary information? | Move decision-critical inventory/action earlier |
| Voice | Does the revision erase useful character? | Keep distinctive observation/cadence |
| Action | Can the reader do the promised thing from their state? | Align steps, eligibility and recovery |

A fictional sentence “Our seamless system unlocks collaboration” is weak because
it omits the mechanism. “Comments stay attached to their revision” says what
happens without asserting that a person reviewed or approved it. Do not convert
a vivid founder story into bland documentation when character serves the medium.

## Make only consequential passes

Fix truth/intent first, then structure and progression, then expression. Select
the passes the problem needs; do not mandate a fixed number or recut sound
material at every pass. Remove repeated sections before tightening paragraphs.
Keep useful anchors, commands, names and meaning-bearing technical terms. Use
[language and rhythm](language-and-rhythm.md) for a targeted wording problem;
its lookup is optional, not a blacklist.

Mark unsupported statistics, testimonials, guarantees, dates and availability
for source resolution. Keep uncertainty where omission misleads. “This helped
our trial” and “this always works” have different evidentiary burdens. Repairs
must meet the same standard as original prose: do not solve vagueness by adding
an unverified actor, schema rule, permission, timing or outcome.

## Reread the result as a whole

Check changed passages with their neighbors and original promise. Does each
section now have a distinct job? Has trimming removed a prerequisite, exception
or voice? Does the reader get enough evidence to act without a duplicate ending?
Read aloud when spoken rhythm matters; inspect rendered layout when formatting
carries meaning, through Code's actual tools.

Code runs literal content/build/link checks where applicable; source review is
not runtime proof. Return the revised artifact, important changes and remaining
truth gaps. No fake expert panel, authorship detector, arbitrary grade or
repeat-until-score loop.
