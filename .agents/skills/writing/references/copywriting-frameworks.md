# Copywriting frameworks

Load when an argument needs a shape. Frameworks help order evidence around a
reader decision; they are not obligatory sections, efficacy claims or templates
to fill when proof is missing.

| Shape | Fits | Main failure |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Problem → consequence → supported remedy | Reader recognizes a painful task | Exaggerating fear or inventing urgency |
| Before → after → mechanism | A real behavior change can be shown | Promise outruns capability/evidence |
| Claim → proof → implication → action | Skeptical reader needs credibility | Evidence arrives too late or proves a different claim |
| Alternative → tradeoff → recommendation | Reader choosing an approach | Straw-man comparison or unsupported competitor facts |
| Task → steps → observable result → recovery | Help/setup content | Hiding action behind a persuasive introduction |
| Observation → tension → lesson | Founder/engineering explanation | Anecdote invents a universal law |

## Select by the missing decision

If a reader already knows the problem, don't spend half the page dramatizing it.
If trust is the obstacle, show relevant proof sooner. If the next action is risky,
explain actual commitment and limits. Combine shapes only when distinct reader
questions justify it; a small factual update may need none.

Example: for fictional version-bound comments, a before/after argument can show
“email thread without version context” then “comment attached to revision.” Its
mechanism must be real. A help guide to recovering an expired invitation should
lead with recovery steps instead, not that product story.

## Headlines and progression

Try a concrete outcome, recognizable obstacle, useful question or surprising
supported observation. Choose the one matching the body and reader; don't force
numbers, contrarian framing or “secret” language. A numbered promise needs actual
distinct coverage, not an arbitrary list. Put the valuable inventory early when
readers need to know what they receive; put deeper reasoning where it helps them
apply it.

Each paragraph/section should add a fact, distinction, proof or action. Transitions
should explain the causal/question relationship, not mechanically announce “next.”
An objection can sit beside its evidence instead of becoming a mandatory FAQ.

Check the whole artifact against its intended finish. Structural neatness cannot
establish truth, taste or persuasion. No universal success percentages or score
loops. Use [copywriting](copywriting.md) for claim discipline and
[language and rhythm](language-and-rhythm.md) for expression.
