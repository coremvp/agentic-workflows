# Search opportunities

Load to choose a useful search-led improvement from query/page signals. Begin
with business relevance and the reader's next action, not a keyword-volume list.

## Establish the actual signal

Retain query/page, dates, country/device where available, impressions, clicks,
position and report limits. Distinguish a reported question from a proposed query,
an external estimate and a current result sample. No impressions in a supplied
report do not establish no demand. Never invent volume, difficulty, revenue or
rankings to complete a priority list.

Compare compatible contexts and periods. A decline may reflect lost availability,
a changed canonical, query mix, seasonality or competition. Zero clicks can reflect
poor position or unsuitable intent, not just a bad title. Aggregates can hide
mixed audiences; inspect the relevant page/query segment before explaining it.
A result sample can reveal prevalent answer types and missing utility, but is
not universal user intent or an instruction to copy competitors.

## Map the reader's job to its owner

Classify the actual question: task, decision, comparison, navigation or recovery.
Find existing canonical answers and read their coverage. Does the page deliver
what the query suggests, from the reader's likely starting state? What supported
explanation, example, limitation or next action would materially improve it?

Overlapping query reports do not prove harmful cannibalization; pages can serve
distinct jobs. Require evidence of genuine answer overlap and a consequence before
recommending consolidation. Useful competing pages may need clearer ownership or
links instead of deletion. Preserve valuable URLs/anchors and hand redirects or
metadata changes to the actual technical owner.

## Choose the smallest supported move

| Situation | Candidate | Evidence needed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Relevant question, incomplete existing answer | Refresh the owner | Supported missing explanation/action |
| Accurate answer, title mismatch | Focused title revision | Query/page mismatch, not CTR alone |
| Broken access/canonical/discovery | Technical fix | Actual route/metadata evidence |
| Distinct valuable question with no owner | Justified new page | Real gap, useful evidence and next action |
| Broad traffic with poor product fit | Defer or narrow | Audience/business relevance |
| Weak signal and no clear useful contribution | Keep current owner or investigate narrowly | A decision-relevant uncertainty rather than a page quota |

For fictional “expired invitation” queries, refresh the recovery guide rather
than duplicate its task under “invitation troubleshooting.” A pricing query
needs the actual offering owner, not a generic article engineered to rank.

Prioritize with concrete reasons: usefulness to a relevant reader, supported
truth, gap, available proof, effort and evidence strength. Compare the strongest
alternative and the no-change option where meaningful. A large topic is weaker
than a small blocked task when we cannot supply a useful answer. No arbitrary
opportunity score, article-length target or automatic publishing quota.

## Define success without forecasting it

Return owner/change, reader job, signal and alternative explanation, contribution,
proof need, next action and uncertainty. Separate immediate correctness (the
answer, metadata or route now matches its intent) from later discovery and useful
outcomes. Establish the available baseline and a meaningful follow-up signal;
visibility changes alone do not isolate the cause of a title or content edit.

Writing refreshes expression; Development owns technical changes under actual
authority. Use the [audit](audit.md) when an upstream defect could explain the
signal. SEO work does not create analytics, vendors, backlink outreach, a monitor
or a ranking promise.
