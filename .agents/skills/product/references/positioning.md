# Positioning

Load when the decision is who this product is for, what alternative it replaces,
or why its supported difference matters. Use existing Product truth; this is
not a new mandatory context document.

## Start from a real situation and workaround

Write a provisional sentence: “For [person in a situation], [product] helps them
[observable progress] through [supported mechanism], compared with [real alternative].”
Treat each slot as a claim to substantiate, not a slogan to publish. A job title
alone is weak targeting: a solo operator reconciling invoices after every trip
has a different need from an enterprise buyer replacing an accounting system.

Inspect the current request, capability owner, audience evidence and alternatives.
Reconstruct the most relevant episode: trigger, task, steps, obstacle, consequence,
workaround and outcome. Distinguish a user's constraint from the founder's
preference. An alternative can be a spreadsheet, manual work, another product,
building it themselves or deferring the problem. Compare what each does in the
same situation; a competitor feature count cannot decide which tradeoff matters.
Verify current external facts before a live comparison.

## Understand why someone would switch or stay

Look for these forces in actual episodes. A missing force stays unknown; do not
fill the table with an imagined customer psychology.

| Force | Evidence question | Consequence for the decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pressure to change | What happened that made the current path inadequate now? | Lead with the relevant trigger and consequence |
| Attraction of the new path | Which supported result or mechanism matters? | Show the concrete difference and proof |
| Habit and existing investment | What works well already, and what would be lost? | Preserve a useful workflow or explain the real tradeoff |
| Anxiety about changing | What risk, setup, trust or commitment blocks a try? | Answer the specific concern or disclose the limit |

Separate “the tool cannot do the job” from “the person cannot discover or trust
the existing path.” The former may need a Product decision; the latter may need
communication, instructions or interaction work. Neither is established by one
vague complaint. Contrast people who switched, stayed or abandoned when that
evidence exists; a satisfied workaround is useful contrary evidence.

Choose a segment by shared situation and consequential constraints, not merely
age, title or positive sentiment. Keep contexts separate when they require
different behavior or promises. Narrow to a useful wedge when evidence is narrow;
broaden only when the shared job and supported behavior warrant it.

## Build an argument that can survive an objection

| Decision | Evidence to find | Consequence for the message |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Who benefits now? | Named task, trigger, current cost/friction | Lead with that situation rather than every possible persona |
| What changes? | Actual before/after behavior and permission boundary | Describe a result the user can obtain |
| Why this approach? | Mechanism that differs from the alternative | Make the difference inspectable, not an adjective |
| Why hesitate? | Switching cost, missing capability, trust or price concern | Answer with evidence or disclose the limitation |
| What should they do? | Available next action and its eligibility | Match the action to actual readiness |

For a fictional tool that attaches comments to a revision, the supported
promise is “Keep comments attached to the revision they concern.” Association
does not establish which version a person saw, whether they completed a review,
or whether they approved it. Seen/read/review-completion claims need their actual
event owner and corresponding evidence; never infer those events from a revision
ID or link. “Approve work without losing the conversation” needs approval
capability too. “Cut review time in half” needs measured outcome evidence.

Test the proposed argument against the strongest current alternative: is the
difference useful enough to justify its cost or disruption in this situation?
An honest limitation can explain fit better than another broad benefit. Do not
manufacture pages for every persona or hide a missing capability behind a slogan.

## Resolve only material unknowns

Infer established audience, pricing, status and rights from host owners first.
When two audience decisions require materially different promises, return to
Lead with one recommended choice, why it fits evidence, its tradeoff and one
question. Continue source inventory while that decision is pending. Do not ask
for a brand workbook or invent missing pricing to finish a draft.

Deliver the recommended situation/alternative, supported difference, strongest
objection and proof, limits and next action. Name the evidence that could change
the recommendation. Marketing selects communication; Writing expresses it.
Product/rights changes require the human's decision.
