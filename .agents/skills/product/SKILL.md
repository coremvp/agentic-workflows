# Product

The user's application and audience supply product truth. Starter defaults do
not establish a business model, brand, demand or customer response. Read the
request, host product/README/configuration decisions and owning implemented
behavior. Infer established choices; ask only when an unknown changes the result.

## Establish the job and current path

Name who is trying to make what progress, in which situation, and what triggers
the work. Read the actual request or episode before inventing a persona. Find
what they do today: existing capability, manual workaround, another tool or
leaving the problem unresolved. What succeeds, what fails, and which consequence
makes a change worthwhile? Separate observed behavior, reported preference and
your explanation; interest in an idea alone does not establish demand.

For a CLI, the useful result may be a correct file and failure exit; for an
internal tool, a resolved operator task. Do not force a purchase, subscription
or growth model onto a library, utility or internal application.

## Make the smallest useful decision

Compare keeping the current path, improving an existing capability and adding
something only when their tradeoffs can change the decision. Prefer one useful
path with clear failures to speculative settings. Ask what can be removed while
the actual job still succeeds. New providers, persistence, abstractions or knobs
need a concrete benefit and an accountable owner, not feature-matrix parity.

Keep the decision bounded in the existing brief or conversation:

- Actor, situation and observable progress; source and current workaround.
- Proposed behavior and why it improves that path, with the main tradeoff.
- What is established, assumed or still unknown; which uncertainty could reverse
  the choice and the smallest evidence that would resolve it.
- Successful result and relevant rejection/recovery cases; exclusions and finish.

Use only the fields the decision needs. A small concrete request needs no forced
PRD, customer study or new context document. A material unresolved choice returns
to Lead with a recommendation, evidence, tradeoff and one human decision.

## Preserve capability and business truth

Inspect actual ownership before proposing a capability: actor/account/object,
authority, operation, effects and consumers; durable truth where it exists.
Do not infer a team entitlement from a user purchase or an access rule from UI.
Product decisions belong to the human; architecture and examples cannot create
new rights or supported capabilities.

Define acceptance through the user's output rather than the existence of a
screen, route or setting. When relevant, include who may act, what happens on
invalid or denied input, what survives a failure, and how the user can recover.
Retain permissions, usable interaction, instructions and meaningful proof where
needed. Pass the accepted outcome to Development; implementation chooses and
checks the actual owner. A product recommendation is not verified behavior.

Marketing and Writing describe only source/evidence-supported behavior and label
unimplemented directions as proposals. If the offering cannot carry a promise,
narrow the promise or return the product decision; persuasive copy cannot add it.

## Conditional depth

Read [Positioning](references/positioning.md) when choosing audience, alternatives,
supported difference, switching motivation or a materially missing buying decision.
Ordinary factual product checks need no positioning exercise.
